# Yamaha VX110 Sport rolling wooden dolly — design draft V1
**Model:** 2005 Yamaha VX110 Sport (Yamaha service manual: 126.8 in length, 46.1 in width, 716 lb dry; not the wet/load-with-fuel weight).
**Output:** vx110-wood-dolly.glb. Named parts can be selected in 3d.fireorbooty.com.

## Dimensions and intent
- Base: 72 in long × 36 in wide (wood). The loaded ski remains approximately 126.8 in long; this saves the trailer tongue/wheel-space footprint, not the length of the ski.
- Two continuous 80 in long carpeted 2x4 bunks, nominal centers 22 in apart.
- Nominal top of bunk: approximately 21 in off smooth level floor *for visualization only*. Measure your actual trailer bunks before cutting posts.
- Bunk spacing, **slope**, pitch and contact locations must be copied from **your loaded trailer** so the VX hull rests correctly with distributed contact. The hull is not included in this model.
- Four 5 in total-lock/dual-brake swivel plate casters, claimed minimum 700 lb rating each, 2,800 lb nominal combined. With only three wheels taking load, ratings sum to 2,100 lb. These are component limits, NOT an assembly load certification. Include cart deadweight and dynamic handling margin.
- Three upright stations per bunk; diagonal braces + 3/4 in plywood cheeks; base is a bolted/braced edge-standing 2x6 assembly.
- Each caster plate supported by 3/4 in plywood and an inner runner plus doubled cross blocking inside the long rail. Fasteners must connect blocking to frame with appropriately specified **structural** connectors; ordinary wood screws into end grain alone are not acceptable.
- Four secure tie-down points on reinforced base. Two removable wheel chocks shown in model.

**Important:** This is a dimensional concept, not a stamped structural plan, proven hull cradle, or approved lifting device. Do not build or load without checking actual bunk geometry, floor conditions, caster mounting hole pattern, load path, weld/bolt/wood load ratings and transfer anchoring with a qualified builder. Never stand under/on/in the movement path of the ski.

## Nominal lumber cut list (verify before cutting)
**2×6** (actual 1.5×5.5 in), five 8 ft boards:
- 2 × 72 in long outer rails.
- 5 × 33 in crossmembers (including two ends, two near the support posts and one center).
- 4 × 11 in inner longitudinal caster runners (two at each end, between each end crossmember and near support crossmember).
- 8 × 7 in transverse caster blocks, in pairs under each caster mounting plate.
Packing example: boards A/B: one 72 in each; C/D: two 33 in + two 11 in each; E: one 33 in + eight 7 in. Saw kerf and trim allowances require checking this last board: 33+8×7=89 in, leaving 7 in. C/D: 88 in, leaving 8 in.

**2×4** (actual 1.5×3.5 in), five 8 ft boards:
- 2 × 80 in bunk rails.
- 6 × upright posts: initial cut about **6.8 in**, but cut only after measuring actual trailer bunk height and chosen caster installed height.
- 6 × approximately 8 to 9 in diagonals; dry-fit and trim angles.
- Spare stock for extra fore-aft bracing, stop blocks and mistakes.

**3/4 in plywood** panel: 4 × 8×8 in caster plates and 6 × approx. 4.6×5 in upright cheeks. Add required structural cleats/brackets after dry-fit. Sealed faces/edges.

### Nominal riser height calculation
This virtual design depicts an installed 5 in caster assembly with **6.3 in plate height**, 3/4 in pad, 5.5 in vertical frame, then post, 1.5 in bunk and about 0.16 in carpet:
**post length (in) ≈ measured target bunk top (in) − 14.21 in**.
If measured bunk top is 24 in, target preliminary post length is ~9.8 in. That does not account for slope, hardware differences, brackets or existing trailer geometry: dry-fit and measure again before final drilling.

## Assembly order
1. With ski ON trailer, measure carpeted bunk height from floor (front and rear), centers between bunks, bunks' inner/outer cant angles, forward and aft contact regions, and garage floor grade. Check if trailer jack lets you align rear bunk ends without unstable trailer positioning.
2. Lay out and square two edge-standing 72 in 2x6 outer rails and five 33 in cross pieces on a flat surface. Fasten the rails with structural joist connectors / through-bolts selected for member direction. Add the four 11 in caster-bay runners and paired 7 in blocking so every caster fixing row passes into sound lumber, not plywood alone.
3. Bolt/reinforce four plywood mounting pads to underlying beams. Drill plate-hole pattern to actual casters. Bolt all four through with fender washers or steel backing plates and locknuts; don't use lag screws only into thin plywood. Verify full swivel arcs clear all bracing.
4. Stand six uprights at the three load-bearing crossbeam stations. Clamp using plywood cheeks and through-bolts. Attach transverse diagonal 2x4 braces and additional fore-aft triangulation as needed against winching/racking.
5. Temporarily fasten the two long bunk beams at trailer-measured spacing and height. Use the trailer's actual cant angle / slope; no 'universal' VX110 bunk spacing is assumed. Ensure no hull fittings/strakes/intake contacts the frame and the weight bears on suitable support surfaces.
6. Wrap bunk rails with one 12-in wide ×12-ft carpet roll sliced lengthwise to yield two 6-in wide, 12-ft strips. Staple on UNDERSIDE, away from hull. Confirm a smooth non-snagging transition where trailer and cart bunks meet.
7. Fit rated tie-down anchors to REINFORCED structural members; chock caster wheels for storage and positively block/tether the dolly before any transfer. Park on smooth level concrete, not soil or gravel.
8. With no passenger, engine OFF and trailer firmly hitched/chocked, use a correctly rated independent fixed anchor and winch in line with an authorized hull eye. Restrain the empty dolly against winch forces with load-rated tie points; caster brakes alone are NOT enough. Keep spotters away from pinch zones and ski's line of travel. Transfer very slowly and STOP if bunks misalign or the hull pitches.
9. Once the full ski weight is on cart and centered over the supports, secure bow/stern to cart, release transfer rig, inspect all joints, then roll slowly on a level floor. Return to trailer by reversing transfer with trailer winch after securing cart and aligning bunks.

## Still needed before a safe actual build
**Three measurements from your real trailer**: (1) height at back of carpeted bunks from concrete, (2) center-to-center spacing and inward cant of bunks, and (3) actual length and position of the hull-contact carpet. Also confirm fuel/gear load and the location of your garage anchor and straight-line transfer space.
